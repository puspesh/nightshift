## What have big technology companies published, and what are the concrete architectural details?

### Takeaway
By late 2026 most large engineering organisations that publish on the topic describe the same shape: an internal "background agent" platform that takes a task from Slack, a ticket or a migration campaign, runs one agent per task in an isolated cloud dev environment that the company already used for humans, fences it with deterministic steps and CI, and hands a pull request to a human. Stripe (Minions), Ramp (Inspect), Shopify (River on Aquifer), Uber (Minion), Spotify (Honk) and Block (Builderbot) are the best-documented; Google, Meta, Amazon and Microsoft publish headline percentages but little architecture.

#### Stripe — Minions (Feb 2026)
- Two-part primary series published Feb 9 and Feb 19, 2026: "Minions: Stripe's one-shot, end-to-end coding agents" [P-snippet] — [Stripe Part 1](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents), [Stripe Part 2](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents-part-2)
- Minions are one-shot, end-to-end agents built on a fork of Block's open-source Goose; the stack is summarised as "goose fork + Devbox + Blueprints + Toolshed" [S] — [rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/stripe-minions); [ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-stripes-minions-ship-1300-prs)
- Agents run in the same per-task devboxes human engineers use; devboxes are pre-provisioned and warmed in a pool. A "10 second" spin-up figure appears in secondary summaries only [S] — [ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-stripes-minions-ship-1300-prs); [Ona newsletter 19 Feb 2026](https://ona.com/newsletter/19-feb-2026)
- Blueprints are workflows defined in code that direct a run. Deterministic nodes ("Run configured linters", "Push changes") never call an LLM; agent nodes ("Implement task", "Fix CI failures") get wide latitude [P-snippet] — [Stripe Part 2](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents-part-2); [Elezea](https://elezea.com/2026/03/toolshed-blueprints-and-why-good-agents-need-good-devex/)
- Toolshed is a centralised internal MCP server with roughly 500 tools; agents get a selected subset rather than all of them, and one source describes a meta-tool for dynamic selection [S] — [lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/stripe-minions-agent-architecture); [Elezea](https://elezea.com/2026/03/toolshed-blueprints-and-why-good-agents-need-good-devex/)
- Context comes from directory-scoped rule files in Cursor rule format, shared across Cursor, Claude Code and Minions [S] — [rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/stripe-minions)
- CI iterations are capped at one or two rounds, then the run goes to a human; sources word the cap differently ("one or two rounds" vs "escalate after two loops") [S] — [victorinollc.com](https://victorinollc.com/thinking/stripe-minions-blueprints); [mrphilgames.com](https://www.mrphilgames.com/blog/what-stripes-minions-get-right-about-coding-agents)
- Scale: 1,000+ merged PRs per week at first disclosure, 1,300+ per week by the Part 2 post (Feb 2026). Every PR is human-reviewed but contains no human-written code; changes are validated against a subset of a 3-million-plus test battery [S, consistent across several] — [ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-stripes-minions-ship-1300-prs); [Port](https://software-factories.port.io/stripe)
- Entry points by May 2026: Slack, Jira, or web [S] — [Ona summit recap](https://ona.com/stories/background-agents-summit)

### Inferences
- The companies with the most detailed architecture posts (Stripe, Uber, Spotify, Shopify) all had strong pre-existing developer platforms (per-task devboxes, DevPods, Fleetshift/Backstage, large test suites). The agent platform is a thin layer on that; critics of Honk make the same point.
- There are two camps: "build a platform around an open or vendor harness" (Stripe on Goose, Ramp on OpenCode, Spotify on Claude Agent SDK, Airbnb's Airchat wrapper) and "buy tools, build the context/governance layer" (Cloudflare, LinkedIn CAPT, Salesforce). Nobody in this set reports writing their own model-facing agent loop from scratch.
- Hyperscalers publish outcome percentages; mid-size, engineering-brand companies publish architecture. The most useful design detail therefore comes from firms with thousands, not tens of thousands, of engineers.

- Stripe, Spotify, Shopify, Ramp, Uber, Cloudflare, Datadog and Block primary posts were not readable from this session (network allow-list). Every figure for them should be re-checked at the linked primary URL.

- No Stripe figure newer than Feb 2026; no Spotify official PR count newer than Nov 2025 was confirmed.

## What have the AI labs published on harness design and long-running agents?

### Takeaway
The labs' 2026 writing converges on "the harness is the product": structured repo knowledge, mechanical enforcement, external state, and independent verification matter more than prompt cleverness, and harness components should be deleted as models improve. Anthropic and Cursor both report that role separation (planner / worker / evaluator) beats flat swarms, while Anthropic and OpenAI both report that the bottleneck has moved to review, CI and source control.

### Cited Findings

#### OpenAI
- "Harness engineering: leveraging Codex in an agent-first world" by Ryan Lopopolo, Feb 11, 2026: an internal product of about a million lines built over five months from an empty repo (first commit late Aug 2025) with zero manually written lines, roughly 1,500 merged PRs, starting with a three-person team [P-snippet] — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/); [Emil Sit notes](https://www.emilsit.net/t/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering/)
- AGENTS.md is kept to about 100 lines as a table of contents pointing into a structured docs/ directory that is the system of record; the "one big AGENTS.md" approach was rejected. Codex wrote the initial AGENTS.md itself [P-snippet] — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/); [lopopolo/harness-engineering](https://github.com/lopopolo/harness-engineering)
- The harness includes custom linters with agent-readable error messages encoding "taste invariants", architecture enforcement, agent access to observability, and recurring background agents that find docs drifting from code and open fix PRs [S] — [claude-code-ultimate-guide evaluation](https://github.com/FlorianBruniaux/claude-code-ultimate-guide/blob/main/docs/resource-evaluations/2026-02-11-openai-harness-engineering.md)
- Working rule: when the agent fails, ask what was missing in the harness, then fix the harness [S, interview] — [Aakash Gupta podcast](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/ryan-lapopolo-podcast)
- Symphony, published Apr 27, 2026 as an open-source spec and reference implementation: an orchestrator that turns a project board such as Linear into the control plane for Codex agents. OpenAI says landed PRs rose 500% in the first three weeks on some teams. Secondary coverage says the core orchestration is roughly 258 lines of Elixir and that it works best in repos that already practise harness engineering [P-snippet/S] — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/open-source-codex-orchestration-symphony/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/28/openai-symphony-codex-orchestration-linear/)

#### Anthropic
- **(2025)** Multi-agent research system (Jun 13, 2025): orchestrator-worker with Opus lead and Sonnet subagents; 90.2% better than single-agent on an internal eval; agents use ~4x chat tokens and multi-agent ~15x; token usage explained 80% of BrowseComp variance; subagents write to external storage and return references; the authors say the pattern fits parallel work and fits most coding poorly [P-read] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- **(2025)** Effective context engineering for AI agents (Sep 29, 2025) and Effective harnesses for long-running agents (Nov 26, 2025) are listed on the engineering index [P-read index] — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering)
- **(2025)** Effective harnesses: an initializer agent creates init.sh, claude-progress.txt, an initial commit and a JSON feature list (200+ features, all failing) and later coding-agent sessions do one feature each. Agents may only flip the `passes` field: "It is unacceptable to remove or edit tests". Browser automation was needed because Claude marked features done after unit tests or curl [P-read] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- Building a C compiler with parallel Claudes (Feb 5, 2026): 16 agents, nearly 2,000 Claude Code sessions over about two weeks, ~2B input and 140M output tokens, just under $20,000, ~100,000 lines of Rust that builds Linux 6.9. Harness is a bash `while true` loop; no orchestrator; agents claim work by committing lock files to `current_tasks/` in a shared bare git repo; "the task verifier is nearly perfect, otherwise Claude will solve the wrong problem"; GCC was used as an oracle to parallelise kernel debugging [P-read] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- Harness design for long-running application development (Mar 24, 2026): planner / generator / evaluator. The evaluator drives the running app through Playwright MCP against hard-threshold criteria; generator and evaluator negotiate a "sprint contract" via files before work starts. Costs: solo run 20 min/$9 vs full harness 6 h/$200 (Opus 4.5); a later Opus 4.6 run cost $124.70 over 3 h 50 min. With Opus 4.6 the sprint decomposition was removed and the evaluator moved to a single end pass; the planner stayed. "every component in a harness encodes an assumption about what the model can't do on its own" [P-read] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- Scaling Managed Agents (Apr 8, 2026): agent split into session (append-only event log), harness (stateless loop) and sandbox, each replaceable. Sandboxes are called through `execute(name, input) → string`; a crashed harness resumes via `wake(sessionId)`; credentials never enter the sandbox (git token used only at init; MCP calls go through a vault-backed proxy). Lazy sandbox provisioning cut p50 time-to-first-token ~60% and p95 over 90% [P-read] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents)
- Redesigning source control at Anthropic (Oct 13, 2026): merges rose from ~2,500/day in May 2026 to 30,000+/day by September; ~25 million CI checkouts per day; main-branch history near 100 GB. Git was replaced for the monorepo by an in-house server-side system (Scion) plus a review tool (Antique) built by seven people in five months. No global merge-queue lock; investment went to post-merge and pre-deploy testing. Reviewers check intent rather than every line [P-read] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control)
- Other 2026 posts on the index, not read: Claude Code auto mode (Mar 25), How we contain Claude across products (May 25), The components of agent memory (Nov 6) [P-read index] — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering)

#### Cursor
- "Scaling long-running autonomous coding" by Wilson Lin, Jan 2026: hundreds of concurrent agents on one project for about a week built a browser from scratch, 1,000+ files and over a million lines. Flat peer coordination with a shared lock file failed; the working design was planners (which can spawn sub-planners), workers, and a judge at cycle boundaries, with a fresh start each cycle to counter drift [P-snippet] — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents); [ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/scaling-multi-agent-autonomous-coding-systems)
- Secondary summaries say GPT-5.2 was preferred for the planner role over Opus 4.5 on long runs [S] — [gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-cursor-scaling-agents)
- "Towards self-driving codebases", Feb 5, 2026: final design is a root planner, recursive sub-planners, and isolated workers on their own repo copies that return a single handoff to their planner; workers do not talk to each other. An integrator agent added to serialise merges and enforce quality was removed. Peak of ~1,000 commits per hour over a week-long run [P-snippet] — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases); [dev.to](https://dev.to/aiengillustrated/how-cursor-got-thousands-of-ai-agents-to-build-one-browser-3loh)
- Lee Robinson (Cursor) on the same work: running agents for weeks "only *kind of* works right now. Still experimental. I wouldn't expect anyone to build a production system like this" — [X](https://x.com/leerob/status/2019460151074259219)

#### Google (Jules, Antigravity)
- Jules is an asynchronous Gemini agent that clones the repo into a cloud VM, proposes a plan for approval, edits, and opens a PR [S] — [respan.ai](https://www.respan.ai/market-map/compare/google-antigravity-vs-google-jules)
- Antigravity **(launched Nov 2025)** has an agent manager for parallel agents in separate workspaces and produces reviewable artifacts (task lists, implementation plans, walkthroughs, screenshots, browser recordings); a 2.0 release is reported for May 19, 2026 [S] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Antigravity); [seroter.com (2025)](https://seroter.com/2025/11/30/go-from-prompt-to-production-using-a-set-of-ai-tools-or-just-one-google-antigravity/)

### Inferences
- Anthropic's own posts show a progression in under a year: bash loop with git lock files (Feb), three-role harness (Mar), decoupled session/harness/sandbox platform (Apr), replacement of git itself (Oct). The constraint moved from "can the agent stay coherent" to "can the infrastructure absorb the output".
- Both Anthropic (managed agents, harness design) and Spotify (LLM judge) report deleting harness parts that a newer model made unnecessary. Harness components have a shelf life of roughly one model generation.
- OpenAI's and Cursor's headline experiments are greenfield repos. Stripe, Uber and Spotify operate on mature monorepos and choose far more conservative, one-task-one-PR designs.

### Gaps
- OpenAI and Cursor primary posts were not readable; the "3.5 PRs per engineer per day" figure sometimes attributed to the OpenAI post was not confirmed in any result.
- Symphony's internals (WORKFLOW.md, per-issue workspaces, retry and proof-of-work rules) were not confirmed from the primary text.
- No primary Google DeepMind engineering post on Jules or Antigravity architecture was found; a "critic agent" in Jules was not confirmed.
- Claude Code agent teams/subagents product docs and Cursor's cloud/background-agent product architecture were not researched in this session.
- A reported Cursor SQLite-rebuild project with a custom VCS is second-hand only ([AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/cursor-s-ai-swarm-rebuilt-sqlite-from-scratch-at-15x-lower-cost)).

## What patterns recur across them?

### Takeaway
Nine patterns show up repeatedly: one task per isolated sandbox, deterministic steps wrapped around agent steps, CI and tests as the real verifier with a small retry cap, a curated tool surface behind an MCP gateway, layered rules files instead of one large prompt, the agent loop and credentials kept outside the sandbox, role separation for long runs, toil and migrations as the first workload, and a human merge gate that is now the stated bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- **One-shot agent in an isolated, pre-warmed dev environment.** Stripe devboxes, Uber DevPods/CI hosts with monorepo pre-checked out, Ramp Modal sandboxes, Jules cloud VMs, Anthropic one Docker container per agent — [ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-stripes-minions-ship-1300-prs); [ZenML on Uber](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-internal-ai-agent-infrastructure-for-software-development-at-scale); [Modal](https://modal.com/blog/how-ramp-built-a-full-context-background-coding-agent-on-modal); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- **Deterministic steps interleaved with agent steps.** Stripe blueprints (lint and push nodes never call an LLM); Shopify Roast (YAML workflow with an embedded coding-agent step, 2025); LinkedIn moving workflow steps into scripts; Datadog's deterministic kernel — [Stripe Part 2](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents-part-2); [Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/introducing-roast); [runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/linkedin-scripts-agent-workflow-deterministic-code); [claude.com](https://claude.com/blog/how-datadog-built-a-universal-machine-tool-for-claude-code)
- **CI/tests as verifier, with capped retries.** Stripe caps CI rounds at one or two; Spotify verifiers block failed changes; LinkedIn requires existing CI plus human review; Anthropic: the verifier must be "nearly perfect" — [victorinollc.com](https://victorinollc.com/thinking/stripe-minions-blueprints); [rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/spotify-honk); [InfoQ podcast](https://www.infoq.com/podcasts/platform-engineering-scaling-agents); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- **Independent evaluator, because self-grading is lenient.** Anthropic's evaluator "talked itself into approving the work" until tuned; Spotify's judge vetoed ~25% of sessions; Cursor used a judge at cycle boundaries; GitHub added a self-review step before showing code — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); [productbuildershq](https://productbuildershq.com/case-studies/spotify-honk/); [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents)
- **Curated tools behind a central MCP layer.** Stripe Toolshed (~500 tools, subset per agent); Uber MCP Gateway; LinkedIn CAPT; Cloudflare MCP access; Spotify's narrow tool set — [Elezea](https://elezea.com/2026/03/toolshed-blueprints-and-why-good-agents-need-good-devex/); [aiproductivity.ai](https://aiproductivity.ai/news/uber-ai-development-inside-look/); [ZenML on LinkedIn](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/contextual-agent-playbooks-and-tools-enterprise-scale-ai-coding-agent-integration)
- **Rules files as a map, not a manual.** OpenAI's ~100-line AGENTS.md pointing into docs/; Stripe's directory-scoped rule files shared across tools; Cloudflare checks AGENTS.md freshness in review — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/); [rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/stripe-minions); [dev.to](https://dev.to/0xgosu/cloudflares-ai-code-review-system-is-an-orchestrator-not-a-chatbot-2jo0)
- **Brain outside the sandbox, secrets outside both.** Anthropic Managed Agents and Shopify Aquifer independently describe a harness outside the sandbox, a durable event log, and a credential-swapping proxy — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents); [Lobu](https://lobu.ai/blog/shopifys-aquifer-in-the-open/)
- **Planner / worker / judge hierarchy for long runs; flat swarms fail.** Cursor (locks made 20 agents perform like 2–3 and made them risk-averse); Anthropic planner/generator/evaluator — [dev.to](https://dev.to/aiengillustrated/how-cursor-got-thousands-of-ai-agents-to-build-one-browser-3loh); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- **External state on disk and in git.** Feature list JSON, progress file, commits (Anthropic 2025); handoff documents (Cursor); docs/ as system of record (OpenAI); session event log (Anthropic, Shopify) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents); [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases)
- **Entry via Slack and tickets.** Stripe (Slack, Jira, web), Spotify (Slack, GitHub Enterprise via MCP), Shopify (public Slack), Block (Slack), OpenAI Symphony (Linear) — [Ona summit recap](https://ona.com/stories/background-agents-summit); [Spotify Part 1](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2025/11/spotifys-background-coding-agent-part-1); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/open-source-codex-orchestration-symphony/)
- **Toil and migrations first.** Uber (~70% toil), Spotify (fleet migrations), Salesforce (endpoint and test migrations), Google (6x faster migration) — [bulletpointpodcast](https://bulletpointpodcast.com/thepragmaticengineer/uber-leading-engineering-through-an-agentic-shift-the-pragmatic-summit/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/salesforce-claims-ai-agents-cut-a-231-day-migration-to-13-days-with-fewer-incidents/)
- **Background entropy cleanup agents.** OpenAI doc-gardening agents; Anthropic's specialised dedup/docs/critic agents in the compiler project — [claude-code-ultimate-guide evaluation](https://github.com/FlorianBruniaux/claude-code-ultimate-guide/blob/main/docs/resource-evaluations/2026-02-11-openai-harness-engineering.md); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)

### Inferences
- The production pattern (Stripe, Uber, Spotify) is deliberately not multi-agent: one agent, one task, one PR, bounded retries. Multi-agent hierarchies appear only in lab experiments on greenfield code and in fan-out review (Cloudflare, LinkedIn).
- AI review is deployed more widely than AI authoring pipelines (Cloudflare, LinkedIn, Atlassian, Uber uReview, Datadog), which fits the shared diagnosis that human review is the constraint.
- "Limited retry rounds" is as much a cost control as a quality control: Stripe's cap is framed as balancing cost and completeness.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much each pattern contributes (for example merge rate with and without blueprints).
- Retry caps other than Stripe's were not found stated as numbers.

## What numbers are reported, and how credible are they?

### Takeaway
Reported figures cluster in three kinds that should not be mixed: merged agent-PR counts (most concrete), agent share of PRs (definitions vary), and "percent of code written by AI" (loosest, usually from executives). Almost all are self-reported and unaudited, several conflict between sources for the same company, and success-rate and cost-per-PR figures are nearly absent.

- Uber: Minion at 11% of PRs (spring 2026), ~15% in a later summary; ~1,800 changes per week; cost up 6x since 2024 — [Ona summit recap](https://ona.com/stories/background-agents-summit); [rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/uber-coding-agents)

### Inferences
- Credibility ranking, highest to lowest: (1) counts of merged PRs through a named pipeline with human review (Stripe, Spotify, Block); (2) platform telemetry (Cloudflare tokens and review runs, Anthropic merges per day); (3) agent share of PRs, which depends on whether "co-authored", "opened by" or "went through" is counted (Shopify, Uber, Ramp); (4) "percent of code AI-generated", which Google itself defines as AI-suggested and human-approved and which includes autocomplete.

## What failures and limits do they report?

### Takeaway
The failures reported are consistent: agents grade their own work too generously, flat multi-agent coordination collapses, verification and review cannot keep up with generation, over-privileged agents cause real incidents, harness scaffolding goes stale within a model generation, and costs climb faster than planned. The largest-scale success stories depend on pre-existing test suites and developer platforms that most organisations lack.

### Cited Findings
- **Self-evaluation is unreliable.** Out of the box Claude "found real issues but talked itself into approving the work" and tested superficially; several prompt-tuning rounds were needed. Earlier, Claude marked features done after unit tests or curl only — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); [Anthropic (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- **Passing tests is not done.** "it is easy to see tests pass and assume the job is done, when this is rarely the case"; new features frequently broke existing ones; the compiler's output is slower than GCC with optimisations off and still needs GCC for 16-bit x86, assembling and linking — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- **Flat coordination fails.** With a shared lock file, 20 Cursor agents delivered the throughput of 2–3 and became risk-averse; the integrator role was a bottleneck and was removed; requiring 100% correctness before each commit was dropped in favour of tolerating a small error rate — [dev.to](https://dev.to/aiengillustrated/how-cursor-got-thousands-of-ai-agents-to-build-one-browser-3loh); [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases)
- **Week-long autonomy is still experimental.** Cursor's own staff say it "only *kind of* works" and is not a production pattern — [X / Lee Robinson](https://x.com/leerob/status/2019460151074259219)
- **Over-specified plans cascade.** Granular technical detail in the planner's spec propagated errors into the build, so the planner was limited to what and why — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- **Multi-agent is expensive and often unnecessary.** Full harness cost 20x a solo run; the evaluator is overhead for tasks inside the model's solo ability; multi-agent uses ~15x chat tokens and suits most coding poorly (2025 statement) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- **Harness parts go stale.** Context resets built for Sonnet 4.5 "had become dead weight" on Opus 4.5; Spotify removed its LLM judge — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/presentations/spotify-ai-codebase-migration-agent/)
- **Infrastructure built for human scale breaks.** Anthropic: "The bottleneck, now, is all of the developer infrastructure built for human scale: source control, builds, and CI"; a global merge-queue lock was not viable. Cursor hit disk I/O limits with hundreds of agents. Coupling session, harness and sandbox in one container lost sessions on container failure — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents); [dev.to](https://dev.to/aiengillustrated/how-cursor-got-thousands-of-ai-agents-to-build-one-browser-3loh)
- **Verification and review are the constraint.** Datadog: agents produce software faster than any team can verify it. Spotify's dataset-migration team still needed repo owners to test manually where automated tests were missing. Stripe keeps human review on every agent PR — [Datadog](https://datadoghq.com/blog/ai/harness-first-agents); [rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/spotify-honk); [Ona summit recap](https://ona.com/stories/background-agents-summit)
- **Agents ignore unstated system constraints and reviewers over-engineer.** Anthropic's agents read the commit log linearly instead of querying the review tool; adversarial review added excess validation; "the role of the engineers was to tell Claude 'no.'" — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control)
- **Permissions and blast radius.** FT-reported Dec 2025 AWS outage (~13 h) after Kiro deleted and recreated an environment; Amazon blames misconfigured access controls; a senior employee reportedly called it the second AI-tool-linked production outage in months — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/20/amazon_denies_kiro_agentic_ai_behind_outage/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/13-hour-aws-outage-reportedly-caused-by-amazons-own-ai-tools-170930190.html)
- **Credentials next to generated code** were an injection exposure in Anthropic's first managed-agent design — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents)
- **Agent-platform side effects.** Over 11,000 PRs received injected "coding agent tip" text from Copilot in Mar 2026; GitHub disabled the feature and called it a logic bug — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/microsofts-ai-slop-is-infecting-github-copilot-is-now-injecting-ads-into-pull-requests)
- **Cost and adoption.** Uber speakers cited costs up 6x since 2024, slower-than-expected adoption, and unsolved productivity metrics — [Ona summit recap](https://ona.com/stories/background-agents-summit); [bulletpointpodcast](https://bulletpointpodcast.com/thepragmaticengineer/uber-leading-engineering-through-an-agentic-shift-the-pragmatic-summit/)
- **Vendor-hosted background agents did not fit a large monorepo**, which is why Uber built Minion on its own CI — [ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-internal-ai-agent-infrastructure-for-software-development-at-scale)
- **Prerequisites.** Commentary on Honk argues it works because of 15 years of platform investment and a predictable codebase; Symphony is said to work best where harness engineering is already in place — [techtrenches](https://techtrenches.dev/p/honk-is-not-magic-its-15-years-of); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/28/openai-symphony-codex-orchestration-linear/)
- **Security is under-served** in the background-agent tooling landscape, per the summit organisers — [Ona](https://ona.com/events/background-agent-landscape)

### Inferences
- The limiting factor reported in 2026 is no longer whether an agent can write the change. It is whether the organisation has a verifier good enough to trust without reading every line, and review/merge infrastructure that scales with agent output.
- Companies that removed a global human or agent gate (Cursor's integrator, Anthropic's merge-queue lock) replaced it with tolerance for small errors plus later testing. Companies operating payment or production systems (Stripe) kept the human gate. Risk appetite, not technology, explains the difference.
- Published failure detail comes mostly from the labs. Company posts are success-framed; their limits have to be read from design choices (retry caps, toil-only scope, mandatory review).

### Gaps
- No company-published post-mortem of an incident caused by a background agent's merged PR was found; the Amazon case is press reporting disputed by the company.
- Stripe's and Spotify's own "what didn't work" sections could not be read; summaries of them are thin.
- No data on reviewer fatigue or rubber-stamping rates for agent PRs at the companies studied. A LinearB finding that AI-authored PRs wait 5.3x longer for human pickup surfaced only as an unattributed search-summary mention with no source URL, so it is not cited above.
- The Background Agents summit sessions (May 2026) probably contain first-hand failure reports, but only an organiser-written recap from a vendor (Ona) was accessible.
